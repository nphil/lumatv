# Panel refresh guard: LumaTV and its test tooling leave LG's OLED compensation alone

Built and host-tested; the helper's guard is off until its TV check passes (`theme.oledcare.refreshGuard`, default false; Settings › System › OLED care › LumaTV panel care › Protect panel refresh). The test tooling's guard is always on.

## The fact it protects

When the TV goes to standby and the panel's counter has passed `offrsLastTime + offrsInterval` (panel hours, interval 4 h), LG runs a compensation (off-RS) of about 10 minutes with the screen dark. A wake in that time aborts it and LG retries at the next standby. Of the 86 standbys from 2026-10-25 to 28, 58 were under 15 minutes (test rounds: `tv_gate.wake()` calls `powerOn`). LG's numbers: `luna://com.webos.service.oledepl/getPixelRefresherInfoList` (`offrsCount`, `offrsLastTime`, `offrsInterval`) and `com.webos.service.panelcontroller/getPanelUsageTime` (`panelUsageTime` in ten-minute ticks, 6 to the hour). The usage call must be sent `{"subscribe":false}`: `{}` fails schema validation (errorCode 3, TV round 16); both the helper and `refresh_guard.py` send it, and their fakes refuse `{}` the same way.

## The rule

A run may be in progress when the TV has been in standby for under 15 minutes, a run was due when the standby began (usage ≥ last run + interval), and the run count has not gone up since. The same rule is in `helper/app/src/refreshguard.rs` (`verdict`) and `compositor/tools/refresh_guard.py` (`verdict`). Anything unknown (LG does not answer, the start of the standby is not known and no reading exists) is not held: a broken reader must not make the TV or the tooling unusable, and the log or the printed line says so.

## What changed

- **Test tooling** (`compositor/tools/refresh_guard.py`, used by `tv_gate.wake()`, so by `tv_gate.py`, `tv_next_slot.py` and `tv_ui_measure.py`): a set in standby is read (luna over SSH) and, if a run is due or likely running, the wake waits until 15 minutes of standby have passed, printing why and rechecking the count every minute (a finished run ends the wait). Where the standby began: the caller (the gate's standby step passes the moment it requested it), else the helper's `panel` topic, else "now". `--force-wake` on all three drivers skips it. The gate's first session timer is 15 minutes longer when its standby check runs (`WAKE_GUARD_MINUTES`), so the set's own safety timer outlasts the wait. `refresh_guard.py status|wait [--force-wake]` runs it on its own. `tv-first-light.sh`, `tv-gate.sh` and `tools/tvslot/r19_checks.py` never wake a set (r19 refuses one that is not awake), and `lumatv apply`'s `turnOnScreen` is the owner's own screen-on, so none of them is guarded.
- **Helper** (`refreshguard.rs`): topic `panel`, `GET /v1/state/panel`: `{read, offrsCount, lastRunHours, intervalHours, usageHours, due, lastStandbyMinutes, standby: null | {since, dueAtStart, countAtStart}, guard: {enabled, deferrals, last}, error, readAt}`. Read at start, at every screen change and every 10 minutes while the screen is on, never polled during standby. The wakes the helper starts are the AI assistant's three that can light a TV in standby: open an app, switch input, press a transport key (`GuardedTv`); while a run may be in progress each is refused with code `refresh_in_progress` and the minutes to wait, logged (`refresh guard: ... deferred`), counted in `guard.deferrals` and shown in the AI audit as a failed call. The owner is never held up: the guard only looks at standby, and a remote key wakes the TV in LG's own code. Nothing else in the helper wakes the TV (the receiver, ambient light, preload and the Bluetooth proxy do not).
- **Settings**: System › OLED care ends with a read-only "Panel refresh" row, "last run N h ago, M runs" (`Helper.panelRefresh`, from the topic; empty and hidden until LG has answered).

## TV check (not run)

1. Reply shape: `luna-send -n 1 -f luna://com.webos.service.oledepl/getPixelRefresherInfoList '{}'` and `.../panelcontroller/getPanelUsageTime '{"subscribe":false}'`. The parser takes the three `offrs` keys at the top or inside a list, numbers or text; it assumes `offrsLastTime` and `offrsInterval` are panel hours on the same scale as `panelUsageTime / 6`. If the keys or units differ, `curl -s http://127.0.0.1:18731/v1/state/panel` shows `read: false` and an `error` naming the key.
2. Screen on: `curl -s http://127.0.0.1:18731/v1/state/panel`: `offrsCount`, `lastRunHours`, `intervalHours` equal LG's, `usageHours` = ticks / 6, `due` agrees with the sum. System › OLED care shows "Panel refresh: last run N h ago, M runs" under the care group.
3. Standby is tracked: put the TV in standby (`gate.sh standby`), after a minute `curl` the topic from SSH: `standby.since` just now, `dueAtStart` as LG's numbers say, `countAtStart` = `offrsCount`.
4. The tooling waits: with a run due at standby, `python3 compositor/tools/refresh_guard.py status` says "a wake would wait N more min"; `tv_gate.wake()` (or `tv_next_slot.py`) prints "the set has been in standby ... waiting until 15 minutes of standby have passed". `--force-wake` wakes at once.
5. The measure that matters: leave a due TV in standby 15 minutes untouched; `offrsCount` goes up by one and `lastRunHours` moves to the panel hour. A wake at minute 3 leaves the count where it was. This also shows whether `offrsCount` changes at the start or the end of a run (the guard assumes the end), and whether the panel hours tick during a run.
6. The helper's guard: `lumatv theme set '{"oledcare":{"refreshGuard":true}}'`, an assistant paired with Open apps at Always (at Ask the prompt cannot be answered on a dark TV and expires first), TV in standby with a run due: the assistant's `launch_app` answers `refresh_in_progress`, `panel.guard.deferrals` is 1, the helper log has the deferral; after 15 minutes of standby the same call goes through. Put the level back afterwards; switch `refreshGuard` on for good only after this passes.
7. Not checked on the set: whether LG's Quick Start+ or Active Standby changes when the run starts; if no run ever starts at standby with the numbers due, `dueAtStart` is true and the guard holds for nothing, the cost being 15 minutes of waiting in the tooling and nothing in the helper.
