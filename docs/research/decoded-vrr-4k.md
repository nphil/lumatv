# Internal 4K VRR: where picture timing survives

**No qualified internal 4K VRR path.** Fixed 4K120 decoding, a 144 Hz display mode, and a variable panel scan for each distinct decoded picture are separate capabilities. For the last one, both the right picture and an independently scheduled scan must be available. HDMI's range is not a measurement of the internal video path.

## What the stock path does

In LG's related O22 `kdriver` source, `core/de/v4l2_vsc_event.c:2204–2209,2224–2235,2266–2276,2411–2418` reads window 0's decoded timing and requests a mode when the source, dimensions, scan type or reported frequency changes. Frequency is derived from the picture's declared rational rate (`chip/o22/de/de_reg_o22.c:3412–3421`), not when it finished decoding. `core/de/v4l2_vsc.c:2434–2463` sleeps 40 ms between passes; there is no multi-sample dwell in that comparison and no hard 40 ms deadline. At 4K120 almost five pictures fit into one sleep. The one-second comparison in `core/de/de_io.c:1003–1009` throttles a **log message**, not a mode change.

For 67 and 72 fps the host selects the same 60 family (`core/de/v4l2_vsc_event.c:2688–2691`), doubled to 120 on this panel class (`core/de/de_io.c:988–999`). Yet [retail measurements](../lumagame-gaming-features.md#odd-rates-and-residue-follow-up-measured) found 67→119.81 and 72→143.84 Hz, at **1440p**, with tracking off. The 144 selection is downstream, not an ARM request for 144. Prior execution of the captured year-24 display-selector instructions (`0x50266a3c`, image SHA-256 `bc5aa595f350a0dc89f6f314c8cfe6977863ab021f7cda133bdf0795bbefd6de`) returned 144 for a stable internal 144 input with readiness zero, and 120 for the same input with forced readiness seven. That synthetic test used **144**, not 72. Neither it nor the 72 measurement establishes the exact 72-family threshold, its hysteresis, or physical lock time. In a separate [50-family transition](framerate-result.md#round-11-when-the-return-to-120-hz-happened), the output stayed at 100 after disconnect and returned to 120 on the next video's new timing; the 50 ms counter samples cannot resolve hardware lock time.

The archived *Game Mode Research* selector receipt (2026-11-01, `vrr-bounded-test-contract.json`) gives a stronger lead than the firmware strings: the normal-mode routine at `0x4001580d–0x4001583f` derives window readiness from **upstream source state**; a source-flag case can produce readiness 6 even when upstream readiness is zero. This does not establish a decoded app that supplies that flag. The source's HFR flag is not evidence for the display firmware's HRR flag. No stock app-level trigger for per-picture internal tracking has been established.

### Matching retail display firmware

The regular boot file `Image.lz4-5.4.268-329.ptl4tv.2` has SHA-256 `3e13cfe557ad8037972c77f03bd4d8d226e2f58eee0127fd38a98ae23fa52ca8`. Its decompressed kernel contains the 1,821,632-byte image 0 at file offset `0x1f71028`, matching the display image hash above. Addresses below are **display MCU virtual addresses** from its startup copy table, not host addresses or evidence of which instructions ran during a game.

- **The display engine receives two timing words.** The routine at `0x502ae3d4`, called at `0x502af621` and `0x502af636`, copies mailbox `+0x3c` to picture state `+0x34` and mailbox `+0x38` to picture state `+0x38` (`0x502ae48a–0x502ae498`). These match current and next PTS in `core/vdec/hal/deipc_reg.h:188–204`. The copy is not restricted to low-resolution pictures: the early height check permits a progressive descriptor (display mode 3) above 1088 lines. Two timestamps do **not** mean two owned picture buffers. No independent release or scan deadline follows from that copy.
- **The current timestamp is used, not just reported.** `0x40018108–0x40018112` compares the previous and current picture's `+0x34`; `0x4001813d–0x40018162` labels those same operands `prevPTS` and `currPTS`. The conditional path updates source-validity state, not the next panel period. Neither this consumer nor the copy establishes a path from next PTS to a scan request.
- **67→120 and 72→144 are fixed-rate selection.** Offline interpretation of `0x50266a3c` with stable source-validity 3, ordinary internal state, doubling enabled and debug overrides off returned 120 at 67.00, 70.99 and 73.00 Hz; it returned 144 at 71.00, 72.00 and 72.99 Hz. At the higher boundary it returned 144 at 142.00 and 145.99, but 120 at 141.99 and 146.00 Hz. Readiness 7 returned 120 at all ten inputs. The selector rounds its hundredths-of-Hz input before testing for 72 or 144 (`0x50266a45–0x50266a77`, `0x50266b61–0x50266b72`); three is a tested state-field value, not a measured three-frame lock time. These are branch thresholds, **not** panel VRR range limits.
- **There is a separate output-side witness.** The controller timing report `0x50269944` has a VRR branch that reads the valid bit and 15-bit vertical total (line count) and prints integer `270000 / total` Hz (`0x502699b4–0x50269a11`). Its other branch only reports ordinary timing. The VRR report samples a changing status word more than once; it is neither an atomic per-scan log nor optical evidence. No nonintrusive per-scan retail app interface to it is established.
- **VRR setup is not a per-picture deadline.** The retail calculator at `0x5026b424` reports clock, input horizontal total, ratios, delay and vertical count (code SHA-256 `a197db64bea8697663583883403bf1962c14404d898195335e2f1faa7424246b`). Its direct caller compares timing geometry and mode against saved values, clears a flag on change (`0x502a0a88–0x502a0b49`), skips the calculator while the flag is set (`0x502a0b8e–0x502a0b90`), and sets it after success (`0x502a0c26–0x502a0c31`). This path configures timing on change; it does not demonstrate that decoded PTS controls individual scans. A separate hardware trigger operating after setup remains possible.

The remaining test is whether a fresh next PTS **and its matching retained picture** are available before the last instant the controller can still schedule that scan. The installed app trace stops before that boundary. A queued timestamp alone, a mode change, or a controller frequency average cannot pass it.

## Routes ranked for the 4K target

| Route | Evidence and decision |
|---|---|
| Stock app / video window | Lowest risk to study. Fixed 4K120 is measured. Above 2,764,800 pixels and integer codec or container rate ≥61, `core/vdec/vo.c:579–602` selects high-frame-rate (HFR) mode. `vds/vdisp_core.c:1078–1093` promotes an exact divisor of 120 or 100 to that family, otherwise keeps the initial rational; **either way** it fixes the timer. `vds/vsync.c:357–369` then ignores per-picture rate changes. A mode lock is not VRR. |
| Separately timed presentation of owned 4K pictures | [INFERENCE] Best development candidate for ≤120 distinct pictures/s: preserve each picture's timestamp and present it later with a fixed delay, independent of the delivery grid. It requires an enforceable surface lifetime, independent scheduling and bounded latency. Neither stock NDL nor the existing display firmware plan provides all three. Offline study is low risk; experimental display firmware is high risk. |
| Display-firmware-only tracking / ForceVRR | The display engine can follow the decoder timer. Retail 70/110 alternation followed the *mean*, not each picture. Switching forced mode off left state behind. Simply changing mode eligibility cannot recover dropped pictures or create an early ready signal. The [display firmware plan](rea-firmware-result.md#de-firmware-modification-route) is not a release path, and the signed decoder's presentation limit is upstream. |
| Decode to GPU / compositor | The documented copy produces 8-bit NV12 rather than native 4K HDR presentation. Stock graphics scanout is 1080p, fixed-grid, without an adaptive-flip interface ([source and measurements](../lumagame-gaming-features.md#decoded-frames-through-the-gpu-plane-source)). Not a 4K substitute. |

**4K144 and low-rate repeats.** The [captured decoder](rea-firmware-result.md) and related `core/vdec/vds/vdisp_core.c:55,264–277` and `vrate.c:565–575` establish the progressive 120 policy, independently of the display selector. A 144 Hz output can repeat pictures; 144 distinct 4K pictures/s is not established. The internal lower tracking bound, the safe transition into low-frame-rate compensation (repeating scans below that bound), and clean handback have not been measured. Neither an HDMI estimate nor an inferred firmware limit is a panel-care specification.

## Cheapest decision before firmware work

`core/vdec/vds/vdisp_core.c:480–505` copies `next_pts` only when another picture is already queued. `:966–978` selects the new frame and updates the display interface **inside the decoder timer callback**. `vds/deif.c:275–276` and `hal/de_ipc.c:275–277` forward PTS and next PTS; they do not announce an independent decode-completion edge. `vds/deif.c:156–165,328–338,578–592,645–655` treats HFR as ready without waiting for a display-read acknowledgement, defaults to two clearance slots and retires old frames. Slot depth is not a supported surface lease.

First check the existing input and diagnostic paths, not the TV. For one 4K stream epoch, keep source picture identity, its original presentation timestamp and tick rate, and local monotonic times for receive, feed acceptance, independently observed selection/availability, and last permitted use. Missing downstream events mean *unobservable*, not zero drops. A constant-rate encoded stream cannot distinguish adaptive presentation from fixed 120 Hz; arrival jitter alone is not variable source cadence.

For a delayed presenter, let `T[i]` be source presentation times mapped to a common clock, `A[i]` the first permitted use and `R[i]` the end of guaranteed ownership. A necessary interval for a constant delay `D` is `max(A[i] − T[i]) ≤ D ≤ min(R[i] − T[i+1] − tail[i])`, where `tail[i]` includes the required time after the next scan starts before the prior surface can be reused. A missing ID, unknown lifetime or unknown tail prevents a positive result. Passing this inequality for a capture does not prove independent scan scheduling, latency acceptability or panel VRR. Those need a matched variable/fixed control and synchronized controller and optical measurements.

For a later **passive mode check only**, an already-planned playback transition plus the existing main-window source report and bracketed `osd_irq` counts distinguishes the 100/120/144 families. No source launch, register access or debug setting is needed for that observation. It cannot prove on-glass per-picture tracking. No decoder runner, firmware load or TV action is part of this finding.

## LumaGame diagnostics boundary

For GFN, `gfn/engine/crates/opennow-streamer-platform/src/runtime.rs:500–508` has the sender's optional frame index, media timestamp and clock rate before pacing. At `:558–585` the VIDEO message gets the TV's monotonic handover time **instead of the media timestamp**. The engine remembers the association for up to 64 in-flight frames. `:387–402,429–440` correlates acceptance using that handover key, not the decoder's selected picture ID. The feeder rounds the key up to load-relative milliseconds, never decreasing it (`gfn/feeder/src/feeder.c:185–191`), then `gf_ndl.c:263–265` passes that integer to the player. Changing only the display firmware would not restore original source timestamps to this argument.

A diagnostics-only capture can join two fixed-size buffers without changing playback or the VIDEO protocol. On the engine side: load epoch, local sequence number, sender index with validity, raw timestamps and clock rate, keyframe/continuity, sink-entry, scheduled-release and actual handover times. On the feeder side: epoch, unrounded handover key, actual player timestamp, complete-message receipt, first feed call, final return, attempt count, queue depth and accept/drop reason (`feeder.c:383–406,1470–1487`). Both processes use the TV's monotonic clock; the sender media clock is a separate domain. Media deltas must account for wrap and discontinuities.

Ten seconds at 120 fps is about 1,200 pictures. Two preallocated 2,048-record buffers of 80 bytes each would cost 320 KiB in total. The capture must drop diagnostic records rather than stall the game, report omissions, and serialize outside the playback path. No stream content is needed. The [existing frame samples](../../game/crates/lumagame-feeder/PROTOCOL.md#app-lifecycle-channel) have arrival gap, feed delay, queue depth and reason, not a downstream per-picture completion. This capture can distinguish source cadence from local pacing; it cannot by itself pass the surface-lifetime or panel test.

### Doom 4K120 capture: where timing changes

Capture `1795325215376`: H.265, 3840×2160, 120 fps, Smooth, 90 kHz sender clock. Doom, HDR and installed build `ba8eb0aad3eaed9d` are operator-reported; both files say build `unknown`. SHA-256 prefixes: streamer `9128ac2bd1c84260`, feeder `1d670c952680c991`. All 1,202 records paired uniquely by epoch and wire key, with consecutive sender IDs 1–1202, identical sizes at both ends, and one successful player call per picture. The only pacing reset was the opening keyframe. No wrap, retry, omission or unmatched row.

Of 1,201 adjacent sender intervals, **1,040** were reproduced within 1 ms at streamer entry, **679** at the scheduled Smooth release, **674** at actual handover and **625** in the player's timestamp argument. Interval correlation with sender timing fell from 0.908 at entry to 0.256 at scheduled release and 0.210 in the player argument. All 1,202 player timestamps exactly equal the ceiling of the load-relative handover time in milliseconds. The 95th percentile absolute interval error was 5.30 ms at the player. These are differences of intervals, not one-way network delays or pixel times.

**Replay, not TV behavior:** keeping the first player timestamp and adding `ceil((RTP[i] − RTP[0]) / 90)` yielded increasing millisecond timestamps with all 1,201 interval errors below 1 ms (95th percentile 0.77 ms, maximum 0.989 ms). One-millisecond precision is not the main loss in this capture; the choice of clock is. This calculation does not establish audio alignment, decoder acceptance of the new timestamps, or a presentation deadline.

**A 120 Hz panel cannot reproduce every sender interval in this sample.** 609 intervals were shorter than 750 ticks (8⅓ ms), including 237 shorter than 625 ticks (a 144 Hz period). Even with pictures instantly available, an ideal 120 Hz presenter that neither drops a picture nor shows it early would lag the desired timestamp by up to 11.10 ms, starting the first scan on time (`scan[i] = max(T[i], scan[i−1] + 1/120 s)`). This is a finite-trace scheduling model, not measured latency. Sender timing is not proof of new game pixels: all 120 pictures ending in the last source-time second carried at most 300 bytes each.

**Next discriminator:** keep the submission/acknowledgement key independent of source presentation time. Before changing playback, check the audio/video clock anchor and reset rules. For panel qualification use distinct tagged 4K pictures with known uneven intervals *inside* the target range (e.g. 10/16 ms shuffled against 13 ms fixed) and a separate ceiling test. Another pacing-off GFN capture may characterize network arrival jitter, but cannot establish picture retention or physical refresh.

### Sender time as the player's PTS (host prototype)

**Clock anchor.** The app has no sender-side audio/video anchor. Audio's wire timestamp is ignored: the feeder stamps each Opus packet with its own load-relative feed time (`feeder.c`, `feed_audio`), after the streamer's jitter buffer has shaped delivery from local arrival time. Video is stamped from its handover time. The only common clock is the TV's CLOCK_MONOTONIC against the feeder's load time. The missing piece is the server's own relation between the two streams: an RTCP sender report (NTP time against RTP timestamp) for each stream, or one shared stream time. Inbound `rtcp1` data is logged by size and discarded (`gfn/engine/crates/opennow-streamer-transport/src/nvst.rs`), the NVST control messages seen are Timer, Termination and FramePacingMode, and the SDP's `mapRtpTimestampsToFrames` is only logged. Whether the server sends sender reports, or puts audio and video RTP on one time base, is unmeasured.

**Mapping** (off by default; `LUMATV_GFN_SENDER_TIME=1`). The engine sends a `VIDEO_CLOCK` message (wire key, low 32 bits of the RTP timestamp, clock rate, gap flag) before each VIDEO. The key stays what ACCEPT and DROP name; with the switch off nothing extra is sent and the player argument is unchanged. The feeder (`gfn/feeder/src/gf_vclock.h`) stamps the first picture of an epoch as it would have been stamped, then adds the sender's elapsed time, rounded up to milliseconds, across the 32-bit wrap. An epoch ends at a load, a flush or hold, a reported gap, an RTP step back, a changed clock rate, or a result more than 30 ms after or 50 ms before the default stamp. The next epoch starts at the larger of the default stamp and the last stamp, so stamps never go back. Audio stays on feed time, so that window is the most the prototype can move video against it, and it holds back sender-versus-TV drift (100 ppm is 6 ms a minute). Taken alone it fits EBU R37's end-to-end limits (sound at most 40 ms before and 60 ms after the picture); today's offset on the TV is unmeasured.

**Result.** The Doom capture replayed through the C mapping (`game/vrr-probe/replay_sendertime.py`): 1,201 of 1,201 intervals within 1 ms of the sender's (625 today), largest error 0.989 ms (9.1 today), 95th percentile 0.77 ms (5.30), no stamp going back or repeating, one epoch (the first picture). The new stamps run 13 ms before to 1 ms after today's, mean −3.25 ms. A one-off host run through the real engine runtime and feeder with the NDL stand-in (not kept in the tree), 60 units on an uneven, wrapping cadence arriving with up to 4 ms of jitter, gave 58 of 59 intervals within 1 ms (switch off: 11 of 59); the miss is a 65 ms start-up stall, which the bound re-anchors.

**Not shown:** A/V alignment on the TV, whether NDL honors the new stamps, panel VRR. **Next, on the TV:** one stream with the switch on and the capture armed, with audio RTP timestamps logged against arrival times. [INFERENCE] If audio and video RTP share a time base, audio's own timestamp becomes the anchor and the local one can go.
